# Demo script self-review

These questions adapt the eight diagnostic lenses derived from the supplied transcript *The Storytelling Genius Of Steve Jobs*. They are practical review prompts, not an official Jobs rubric or a validated scorecard. Consider each question, then choose only the lenses that improve your script. A demo does not need every technique.

Before reviewing, name your audience, intended takeaway or decision, available time, and any wording or delivery constraints. Keep what already works; usually three to five meaningful edits are enough.

## Eight questions

1. **Analogy:** Would a familiar comparison make an unfamiliar idea clearer for this audience while preserving the product's limits?
2. **Concretization:** Can the audience picture the person, task, action, and change behind the major claims, using details the material actually supports?
3. **Demonstration:** For each central promise, what action and visible result would let the audience assess it? If that proof is unavailable, what narrower promise can I honestly make?
4. **Rapport:** Does the script start from the audience's goals, expertise, and constraints, and guide a discovery without demanding admiration or a particular reaction?
5. **Framing:** Have I given a credible reason this change matters now, acknowledging what already works without inventing an inevitable future?
6. **Redefinition:** Would a clearer interpretation of the problem or category help this audience see value that the demonstrated behavior supports?
7. **Soul:** Is the human stake believable and supported by the scenario, expressed in language I would naturally use without a manufactured story or forced disclosure?
8. **Showmanship:** Which meaningful result should the audience remember, and would a clearer setup, sequence, pause, or reveal help them notice it?

## Check the central promise against the evidence

For the most consequential beat, put **What you say / What you do / What the audience sees** side by side. Do the words claim only what the action and visible result can establish?

- Separate supported facts, proposed demonstrations, and unverified work. An instruction in a script is not proof of successful execution or rehearsal.
- Keep supplied evidence labels and qualifications intact, including distinctions between live, recorded, prototype, simulation, illustration, prepared, planned, and unverified. Reserve live-verification claims for actual verification evidence.
- Check the end result: a recommendation is not execution, a draft is not delivery, and a prepared screen is not proof of a working integration.
- Do not invent facts, anecdotes, metrics, ROI, capabilities, integrations, or completed outcomes. Qualify, support, or remove unsupported claims already in the script. Mark new scenarios as illustrative and new actions as proposed.
- Preserve exact required wording, attributed quotations, and scope limits. Flag a conflict and adjust surrounding context instead of silently changing protected language.

Choose the edits that most improve meaning, credibility, and sequence. Give each a clear location and usable replacement. If adding a beat, identify what to shorten or remove and any preparation it needs. Treat timing as an estimate until rehearsed.
